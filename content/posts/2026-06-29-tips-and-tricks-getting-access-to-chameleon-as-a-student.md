<p>One of the most common questions we get from students discovering Chameleon for the first time is some version of: "I created an account — now how do I actually get access?" If you've tried to apply for a project and hit a wall, or simply aren't sure where to start, this guide is for you.</p>

<h3>Why you "probably" can't create your own project</h3>

<p>Chameleon resources are accessed through <a href="https://chameleoncloud.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/project.html"><strong>projects</strong></a>, and each project requires a Principal Investigator (PI) who is responsible for the allocation. PI status is limited to faculty and research staff at US-based academic institutions — see the full <a href="https://chameleoncloud.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/pi_eligibility.html">PI eligibility requirements</a>. Most students — undergraduate through PhD — aren't eligible to apply as PI on their own.</p>

<p>There is one notable exception: <strong>NSF Graduate Research Fellowship recipients</strong> can apply for a Startup allocation as PI. If you hold an NSF GRF award, you can include your grant number or award letter as part of a project application to qualify.</p>

<p>For everyone else, the options below are the right paths forward.</p>

<h3>Option 1: Join a PI's existing project</h3>

<p>This is the most common way students access Chameleon. If you're working with an advisor, professor, or research mentor who already has a Chameleon allocation, ask them to add you to their project or create a new one for you. There's no separate approval process — once your PI adds you, you have full access to the system under their allocation.</p>

<p>If your advisor isn't already on Chameleon, it may be worth bringing it up with them. They'll need to <a href="https://chameleoncloud.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/pi_eligibility.html">request PI status</a> and then <a href="https://chameleoncloud.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/project.html#creating-a-project">apply for a project</a> — both are straightforward, and decisions typically come within one business day.</p>

<p>Your PI has a few ways to <a href="https://chameleoncloud.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/project.html#adding-members">add you</a>:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Direct add</strong>: They can add you by your Chameleon username or email address from the Project Members section of their project dashboard.</li>
  <li><strong>Join link</strong>: They can generate a shareable join link. When you click it and sign in, you're automatically added to the project — useful for adding multiple people at once.</li>
  <li><strong>Email invitation</strong>: If you haven't created a Chameleon account yet, your PI can invite you by email. You'll receive a link to create an account and be added to the project in one step.</li>
</ul>

<p>If you don't have a Chameleon account yet, <a href="https://chameleoncloud.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/federation.html">create one first</a> — account creation uses federated identity through Globus, so you can likely sign in directly with your institutional credentials. Once logged in, your username appears in the top-right corner of the portal and on your profile page. Full details on the project membership process are in the <a href="https://chameleoncloud.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/project.html">Project Management documentation</a>.</p>

<p>This same path applies if you're taking a course that requires cloud resources. Chameleon regularly supports large classes across a wide range of CS topics — from introductory cloud computing to advanced subjects like MLOps and distributed storage systems. If your course might benefit from system access, it's worth encouraging your instructor to set up a project for the class. They follow the same process as any other PI, and once the project is in place, they can add all enrolled students at once.</p>

<h3>Option 2: Daypass</h3>

<p>If you want to explore Chameleon without an allocation — or just reproduce a published experiment — <strong>Daypass</strong> is the easiest path. It lets anyone request temporary access to system resources for the purpose of reproducing an experiment published on <a href="https://trovi.chameleoncloud.org/">Trovi</a>, our experiment sharing platform. No allocation, no project membership, no approval from a PI required.</p>

<p>This is a great option if you're evaluating whether Chameleon is the right fit for your research, or if you want to run through an experiment from a paper before committing to a full project. Learn more in our <a href="https://www.chameleoncloud.org/blog/2022/01/24/interactive-science-made-easy-with-chameleon-daypass/">Daypass blog post</a>.</p>

<h3>Still not sure what applies to you?</h3>

<p>If you've read through the options above and none of them quite fit your situation, the best next step is to check our <a href="https://chameleoncloud.org/learn/frequently-asked-questions/">FAQ</a> or <a href="https://www.chameleoncloud.org/user/help/ticket/new/guest/">submit a help desk ticket</a> — you don't need an active allocation to do so. We're happy to help you figure out the right path — student access questions are some of our most common tickets, and we'd rather help you get started than have you give up before you've had a chance to use the system.</p>
